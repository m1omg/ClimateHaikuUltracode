    Glossary

    Absorbed sunlight
    The starlight the planet keeps: (1 minus albedo) times the star's flux, divided by four. Measured in W m-2.
    Albedo
    The share of sunlight reflected back to space by clouds, ice and the surface. A bright planet absorbs less and stays cooler.
    Cap (moist limit)
    The most infrared a moist atmosphere can radiate, about 282 W m-2 in clear-sky models. If more sunlight is absorbed than this, no equilibrium with liquid water exists.
    Greenhouse effect
    Gases that absorb infrared send some of it back down, so the surface must be warmer to radiate the same energy to space.
    Hysteresis
    The state depends on the path taken. A snowball that has formed may need more CO2 or more sunlight to thaw than it took to freeze over.
    Lag
    How far a slow reservoir, such as the deep ocean or the carbonate cycle, is from the equilibrium the present conditions would give. A settled reservoir has caught up.
    Moist greenhouse
    A liquid ocean with enough water vapour high in the air for hydrogen to escape. The ocean drains over about a billion years.
    OLR (outgoing longwave radiation)
    Infrared the planet radiates to space, in W m-2. At equilibrium it balances absorbed sunlight.
    OED (ocean-equivalent depth)
    All the water spread evenly over the whole planet, as a depth. Earth's ocean is about 2.7 km deep in this sense.
    Runaway
    Absorbed sunlight exceeds the cap, so no balance exists. The surface warms until the infrared limit binds, then the surplus energy is spent on evaporation and the surface temperature holds.
    Snowball, slushball, eyeball
    Frozen states. A snowball is ice over the whole ocean. A slushball has an open tropical band. An eyeball has a small open equatorial sea between polar ice caps.
    Stellar flux S
    Starlight at the planet relative to present Earth (S = 1). Brightening means S rises as the star ages.
    Weathering
    Rock weathering removes CO2 from the air and speeds up when the planet is warmer. It is a slow thermostat, on a timescale of about 240 kyr.

    This is a zero-dimensional model. It has no latitude structure and no cloud feedback. Read the caveats in the README before quoting numbers.
